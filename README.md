# Slurry Lab

A first-person chemistry-lab puzzle game set in a world with much simpler chemistry than ours. You walk around a lab handling fluids in glassware, and the goal is to work out how to synthesize more of a scarce target fluid.

**Premise** (see the intro): you're Nadia Hassan, a passenger on a Celestia Starlines colony ship to Mu Ceti, woken from cryosleep after something hit the ship and took out the bridge, the crew and nearly all the cryostabilizer. The 32,210 passengers still asleep need 0.000035 mL each a day of it to last the 4,456 days (12.2 years) left: 5.023 L; there are 2.811 L. The target species is the cryostabilizer, so you have to figure out how to make more.

The faucets are unlimited, so the target is scarce because you don't know how to make it, not because you lack raw material. The puzzle is discovering a synthesis route.

## The world's physics

### Fluids

A packet of fluid, such as the contents of a flask, is fully described by:

- its **temperature**, and
- its **count of each molecular species**.

There is **no solvent**. Reaction rates depend on mole fractions, so only ratios matter, not absolute amounts. Adding fluid gradually still matters, because it keeps one reagent scarce while it goes in. There is no "add water" lever.

Volume is measured in molecules, so a flask's capacity is a number of molecules: a billion. Capacity, fill level, pouring and flow all go by molecules, so bonding shrinks a fluid and breaking bonds swells it, and whatever no longer fits spills over the rim. A checkbox in the Chemistry panel counts atoms instead. Mass, heat capacity, reaction rates, the god-mode percentages and the receptacle's purity test stay per atom either way. The setting isn't saved, and *Restore defaults* turns molecule counting back on.

Every count in a vessel is a whole number of molecules, and its heat is a whole number of energy quanta (one unit of bond energy each). Temperature is derived as heat / (heat capacity × atoms), so mixing simply adds heat, and energy is conserved exactly, except what the heater adds and what's lost to the room (see **Heat**). Whenever the expected result is fractional, such as reaction events in a step, one species' share of a pour, or the separator's split, it's rounded at random: up with probability equal to the fraction. That keeps averages exact, so slow reactions still happen. JavaScript numbers hold whole numbers exactly up to 2⁵³ ≈ 9×10¹⁵, so plain numbers are enough and BigInt isn't needed. Faucet recipes are the one fractional thing: one atom's worth, poured out as whole molecules. Counts that large behave like continuous quantities, so no small-number artifacts show up. The chemistry doesn't care about the scale, since everything in it depends on ratios.

### Atoms and molecules

- **Six atoms:** the primary and secondary colors R, G, B, C, M, Y.
- **Three opposed pairs:** R/C, G/M and B/Y. Each pair is a *group*.
- **What a molecule is:** a connected graph of atoms with at most one atom from each group. No molecule can hold two Rs, or both R and C. A molecule therefore has at most three atoms.
- **Species count:** 6 singles + 12 pairs + (8 color-triples × 4 connected shapes: 3 paths and a triangle) = **50 species**. That is small enough to simulate exactly.

### Bonds

Each of the 12 bondable color pairs has three parameters:

- `E`: the bond energy. Positive means stable.
- `Ea`: the activation energy.
- `A`: the Arrhenius prefactor.

Opposite colors never bond. A species' energy is `U = −Σ E` over its bonds.

### Reactions

Reactions run on sim time with Arrhenius kinetics. Units are `k_B = 1` and `T_room = 1`.

`Ea` is measured as the barrier's height **above the higher of the two states**. For a reaction with energy change `ΔU`:

- downhill rate = `A · exp(−Ea / T)`
- uphill rate = `A · exp(−(Ea + |ΔU|) / T)`

This gives detailed balance automatically, and `Ea = 0` is always legal. As a rule of thumb, a barrier of 5 is slowish (e^−5 ≈ 1/150 of A), a barrier of 15 is frozen, and a bond with `E = 8` is essentially permanent at room temperature but breaks readily at `T = 4`.

The reaction types are:

1. **Formation** (bimolecular). Two molecules with disjoint groups join with one new bond. The rate is `k · x_a · x_b`.
2. **Ring closure** (unimolecular). A 3-atom path becomes a triangle.
3. **Breaking** (unimolecular). The reverse of 1 and 2.
4. **Swap** (bimolecular, `Ea = 0`, one global prefactor). Any two species holding opposite colors in the same slot trade those atoms, for example `RG + C ⇌ CG + R`. Swaps between bound atoms are allowed too. A swap relabels atoms but keeps the bond topology. So swapping is fast recoloring, while formation is the slow construction of a shape, and the target's shape still has to be earned through formation.

### Heat

- **Reaction heat:** exotherms heat the fluid through a per-atom heat capacity.
- **Mixing:** heat adds, so temperatures average, weighted by atom count.
- **Heating:** besides exothermic reactions, the resistive heater (see **Tools**) heats whatever runs through it.
- **Cooling** (`src/game/cooling.ts`): fluid in every vessel (flasks, tanks, funnels, the spectrometer's cup, the heater's tube, hose funnels) drifts exponentially to the room's temperature, T = 1, from above or below. Each sim second it closes 1 − e^(−rate · exposure) of the gap, where rate is 0.1 and exposure is a rough thermal surface per volume worked out from how the fluid stands as drawn: h deep, and w wide on average (its drawn area over its depth). Taking the fluid to be as deep front to back as it is wide, its floor and free surface give 2/h and its walls 4/w. So a thin layer, or a narrow column, loses heat fast, and a deep, wide pool slowly. A flask fills its wide base first; a tank fills straight up, a funnel up from its narrow stem, and a pipette its tube and then its cup; the heater's tube holds a shallow stream along its floor. All but 1/e of the gap goes in about 65 s for a full flask, 110 s for a full dispenser, 27 s for a full pipette, and 9 s for a tenth of a flask lying 2 deep across a dispenser's floor. Fluid in mid-air (streams, drops) doesn't cool. Faucets pour at T = 1, so faucet fluid just sits. Besides waiting, the ways to lower a temperature are diluting with faucet fluid, running endothermic reactions, or passing it through the heat exchanger against something cooler. The Chemistry panel's *Cooling* rows edit the rate and the ambient temperature; they aren't saved, and *Restore defaults* puts them back.

### Appearance

- **Color:** by default, a fluid looks like the atom-weighted mix of its colors, and the bond structure is invisible. This is lossy on purpose: R + C averages to grey, for example. (The Appearance panel can try out colors that depend on bonds; see below.)
- **Temperature:** each effect is a smooth function of T, with no cutoff where one effect takes over from another (see `src/game/appearance.ts`).
  - **Cold** fluids fade toward black. The color's brightness is scaled by `1 − e^(−3T)`: black at T = 0 and almost full brightness by T = 1.
  - **Hot** fluids glow. The glow's strength is `ln(1+T) / ln(101)`, and it drives the size and opacity of a corona and a wider halo around the flask. At high T it also bleaches the fluid itself toward white.
  - The glow is subtle but visible at T = 1, obvious at T = 10 and nearly blinding at T = 100.
  - God mode shows the actual number.
  - The **Appearance** debug panel tunes every constant in these curves live. *Copy values* puts the current settings on the clipboard as JSON, and *Restore defaults* undoes your changes.
- **Composition, experimentally:** the Appearance panel can also make color depend on bonds, so reactions show. A fluid is then the atom-weighted average of its molecules' colors, and each molecule's color comes from one of three models:
  - *current*, the default: a molecule is the average of its atoms, so the fluid is the average of its atoms and no reaction changes it;
  - *1: paint*: a molecule's atoms multiply like paints, rescaled to their average's brightness, so C–Y is green and M–Y red;
  - *2: light*: a molecule's atoms add like overlapping spotlights, clipped, so R–G is bright yellow and △RGB white.

  *Mix* blends between the average (0) and the full model (1). The *Cloudiness* checkbox makes bigger molecules more opaque: the fluid's opacity is the atom-weighted average of an α for singles, pairs and triples.

## The puzzle's chemistry

Blue bonds are uphill (E = −1 for now) and have no prefactor (A = 0), so they never form or break on their own, at any temperature. Blue gets into a molecule, or out of one, only by swapping places with yellow, its opposite color. The target, the **△RGB triangle**, sits above R–G + B, so it never dominates an equilibrium.

Yellow bonds have a high barrier (Ea = 10), so at room temperature they hardly form or break, and it takes heat to bond yellow to anything or free it. That keeps a trace of yellow from eating the target. Yellow still swaps in for blue freely (swaps have no barrier), so each yellow atom spoils one triangle into △RGY. But that ring can't open cold, so the yellow stays stuck there instead of moving on to the next triangle. Hot, it does move on, and the damage keeps growing.

The four faucets of pairs with blue pour them whole, since with A = 0 they never come apart. No faucet holds atoms from all three groups, so none pours any triangle: the target has to be made.

- **Overflow:** fluid pouring into a full vessel (from a spout, a drop, a faucet, a hose or your own pouring) still goes in and mixes, and the same amount of the mixture spills over the rim: off the right of a flask's lip, or over the right lip of a tank or funnel, as it faces. It falls as a stream, into the first open top below, which may overflow in turn, or down the drain. So a flask of blue under a stream of red turns redder and redder while purple, then red, pours off it. Faucets keep running into a full vessel, too. Chemistry that swells a fluid (by molecules) spills the same way.
  - **Dispenser:** one tank, drained through one spout.
  - **Tank:** a big dispenser: one tank holding a hundred flasks, drawn as big as a hundred flasks are (560 wide and 397 tall, in world units where a flask is 70 tall, and 40 deep), with a valve and spout under a sump at the bottom middle. Ten thin, faint lines across it mark each tenth of its height, the last at the brim. Like any tank it pours 1 flask/s full and fully open, in proportion to how high its fluid stands.
  - **Pipette:** a narrow dispenser holding a tenth of a flask, filled through a little funnel on top (whose mouth is what catches streams and drops, and spills over its right lip when full). Its fluid is drawn across tube and funnel together, by area, so it fills the tube first and a full pipette's funnel is full to the brim. Ten graduation marks up its left wall show each tenth of its capacity: evenly up the tube for the first seven, then closer together up the widening funnel, the last at the brim, with the half and the full marks longer. Full and fully open, its valve lets out 0.02 flask/s, a fifth of what it holds each second. Its fluid's height, which sets that flow, runs up its narrow tube and then its cup, so the flow drops off quickly while the cup drains, then more slowly down the tube.
  - **Separator:** one tank, drained through a manifold with five spouts, 72 apart so a flask or a tool's tank fits under each. It sorts molecules by their mix of primary-color atoms (R, G, B) and secondary ones (C, M, Y), and each has its own spout, left to right: all primary (R, R–G, △RGB), two in three (△RGY, R–G–Y), half (R–Y), one in three (△RMY), all secondary (C, C–M). By default it's perfect: every molecule goes out its own spout. Below that, a molecule goes to spout j in proportion to e^(−sharpness · (j − its own)²), so at sharpness 3 it goes 91% out its own spout if that's one of the middle three, or 95% at either end, and nearly all the rest out the spouts next to it. The Chemistry panel's *Separator* slider sets the sharpness from 0 (an even five-way split) to 10, the default, which is perfect, showing what share each spout keeps; it isn't saved, and *Restore defaults* puts it back. It splits whole molecules, and heat goes with the atoms. A small handle on one end of the manifold shows which way round it is (it starts on the left), without saying which side gets what.
  - **Heat exchanger:** two tanks, A and B, whose streams pass each other in counterflow through two hoses wound into a double helix. Each stream crosses over and leaves from the spout under the *other* tank, and the hoses show each stream's color. They trade heat but never mix, and heat is conserved. It uses the standard effectiveness–NTU model with a fixed exchange capacity (`EXCHANGE_RATE`, 2 flasks/s): the slower stream gets a fraction ε of the way to the other's inlet temperature. Two equal streams at 0.25 flask/s (valves at a quarter, tanks full) nearly swap temperatures (ε = 8/9). At 1 flask/s they get ε = 2/3. If one valve is shut, the other stream passes through unchanged.
  - **Splitter:** a quarter-flask funnel that drains straight through (2 flasks/s when full, and in proportion to how high its fluid stands, like a valve) into a fork with two spouts, as far apart as the separator's. Its valve doesn't open or close: it sets the split. Point the lever left to send everything left, right for everything right, and anywhere in between for a share in between, straight up being even. Below the valve it goes to the nearer side. It starts even, and anything arriving faster than it drains overflows.
  - **Size sorter:** a quarter-flask funnel with no valve that drains (2 flasks/s when full, and in proportion to how high its fluid stands) down a sloping chute, like the screens of a gravel sorter. Each of three spouts, as far apart as the separator's, sits under one hole, in order down the chute. The chute looks plain all the way down, so the player has to find out what each hole takes. By default it's perfect: singles all fall through the first screen, pairs the second, and triples go off the end of the chute. Weaker, it leaks: each size goes 1/3 + 2/3 · strength out its own spout and the rest evenly out the other two. At strength 0.55, 70% of each size goes out its own spout and 15% out each of the others. The Chemistry panel's *Size sorter* slider sets the strength from 0 (every size splits evenly three ways) to 1 (perfect), showing what share each size keeps; it isn't saved, and *Restore defaults* puts it back. It splits whole molecules, and heat goes with the atoms. The film on the chute shows what's still sliding down.
  - **Resistive heater:** a long horizontal glass tube with a copper wire coiled down its middle. A quarter-flask funnel over its left end drains into it at 1 flask/s when full, and in proportion to how high its fluid stands (anything arriving faster than a full funnel drains overflows), and the tube carries whatever is in it to the right end in 6 s, however much there is, and down a spout there. Three taps along its underside, at the ends of the tube's first three quarters, each a valve over a spout as far apart as the separator's, let fluid out sooner: a tap's valve lets out up to 1 flask/s of what's passing, so a wide-open one takes everything and none goes further. A dial on a box above the tube's right end sets the wire's temperature: off at 0, then rising geometrically from room temperature (T = 1) to 100 at full, so a quarter turn is about 3 and halfway 10. The wire goes from copper through glowing red, orange and yellow to white-hot as the dial turns up. Fluid in the tube closes 1 − e^(−0.25 t) of the gap to the wire's temperature in t seconds, so by the taps it's 31%, 53% and 68% of the way, and 78% by the end (less what the tube loses to the room meanwhile, see **Cooling**), though a little spreads out ahead and behind as it goes (the tube is simulated as 24 well-mixed stretches). The wire only heats, so fluid hotter than it passes through unchanged, and with the dial off nothing is heated. Reactions go on in the tube as anywhere else. What's in each stretch is drawn lying along the tube's floor, as deep as that stretch holds when the funnel feeds it flat out. Its feed rate, transit time, heating rate and top wire temperature are editable in the Chemistry panel's *Resistive heater* rows; like the drip settings, they aren't saved, and *Restore defaults* puts them back.
  - **Receptacle:** there's exactly one, every lab has it, and it stays where it is: it can't be picked up, carried or put away (an arm holds it, like everything else), and older saves get one (between the last two flasks on the shelf, under the spectrometer, unless a preset places it). It's labeled "cryostabilizer receptacle" above, like the reference. It's a vessel holding 210 flasks (a little over twice the reference, so the reference alone can't fill it) on a cabinet with a row of six small lamps, a verdict lamp, a red button and a small green-on-black screen like the spectrometer's, which shows the litres it has taken so far of the litres needed ("3.373 L / 5.023 L"), with a hose running from the cabinet's foot straight down to the floor or counter below, and a reject valve beside it over a spout. The reject valve has no handle, since the player can't work it: only the receptacle opens it. Pressing the button lids the vessel and starts a cycle, in sim time like everything else (so pausing freezes it):
    1. For 1.6 s it thinks: the lamps blink in a random pattern, each with a computer blip at a random high pitch.
    2. If what it holds is more than 95% target by atoms, a happy rising pair of tones plays, the verdict lamp lights green, and it flushes everything down the hose with a rushing sound, evenly over 1.5 s; the hose shows the fluid going. The target atoms flushed count toward the goal.
    3. Otherwise a low error buzz plays, the verdict lamp lights red, and the reject valve opens and pours everything out the spout, evenly over 2.5 s, into whatever's below it or down the sink.

    Then the lid lifts. The verdict lamp stays lit until the next press, and the button does nothing mid-cycle. An empty vessel is refused.
  - **Flow meter:** there's exactly one. Like the spectrometer, every lab has it (it starts between the cryostabilizer reference and the heat exchanger, unless a preset places it), it isn't on the hotbar, and it can't be put away; older saves get one. It's a quarter-flask funnel with no valve, draining straight through a small cabinet (at 2 flasks/s however full it is, unlike the other funnels, so what it shows is what's arriving; anything faster overflows) and out one spout. The cabinet's yellow-green seven-segment display, unlabeled like the scale's, shows what's flowing out in millions per second (of molecules, or atoms if volume counts atoms), to as many decimal places as its four digits leave room for: 0.012, 56.78, 2000. The reading is averaged over about a sim second (it closes 1 − e^(−t/τ) of the gap to a new flow in t seconds, τ = 1 s), so a steady drip reads as a steady trickle; τ is editable in the Chemistry panel's *Flow meter* row, not saved, and *Restore defaults* puts it back.
  - **Cryostabilizer reference:** drawn flask-sized but holding a hundred flasks, sealed on top, with a valve on the bottom whose fully open flow is only 0.02 flask/s when full. Nothing can be poured, dripped, piped or fauceted into it. It's labeled "cryostabilizer reference" and holds the game's starting target (in *Stranded*, full: a hundred flasks of triangles, 300 billion atoms, hanging over the fourth flask). Like the spectrometer it's one of a kind: it isn't on the hotbar, it can't be put away, and a save keeps at most one. Unlike the spectrometer, a lab doesn't have to have one.
  - **Mass spectrometer:** there's exactly one. Every lab starts with it (at the right of the back wall, unless a preset places it), it isn't on the hotbar, and it can't be put away. Older saves with none get one, and with several keep the first. It's a sample cup (a thousandth of a flask, 1M, so even one drop can fill it) on a deep cabinet with an old green-on-black screen and a red push button on its front, with no words anywhere. The screen has three hexagons, for molecules of 1, 2 and 3 atoms, left to right. Each hexagon is split into six sextants, one per color, clockwise from the top: G, C, B, M, R, Y. There are no lines between the sextants, just their glow. The button reads the sample. Then a lid drops onto the cup and the sample drains away into the cabinet, evenly over the run, so the cup is empty just as the run ends and the lid lifts. Running it is the way to empty the cup. While the lid is on, nothing can be poured in, and drops and streams fall past it. Each sextant then glows as bright as the share of the cup filled by molecules of its size with an atom of its color in them, so a fuller cup reads brighter. A full cup of R lights 1R fully, and half a cup of R half as bright. A full cup of R–G lights 2R and 2G fully, and a full cup of △RGB all three of its sextants. A full cup of 20% R, 20% G, 30% B and 30% R–G lights 1R and 1G at 0.2, 1B at 0.3, and 2R and 2G at 0.3 each. The more mixed the sample, the dimmer and harder to read the screen. A run takes a while, in sim time like everything else (so 4× and 16× speed it up, and pausing freezes it, silent and still), in three phases. The cabinet rumbles and shakes, and a lamp blinks. Each phase ends by lighting its hexagon (at 1, 3 and 7 s) with a short, quiet, high C-major chime, and the next phase rumbles a step louder, higher-pitched and shakier, holding steady until it ends. After the third it falls quiet. The reading stays up until the next run (and is saved), and the button, which stands proud of the cabinet and lights up under the crosshair, stays pushed in (nearly flush and darker) and does nothing while a run is under way. A spectrometer has no spouts and no valve. Its sounds (`src/game/rumble.ts`) are synthesized with Web Audio. For now the readout is a stand-in for something more physical.
  - **Hose:** a funnel inlet and a nozzle outlet, each picked up and hung anywhere on its own, with a magic pump between them (up to 2 flasks/s) and a translucent tube drooping between them that shows what's flowing. A new one comes in your hands by its funnel, with its nozzle trailing off to the right; let go, and from then on each end is picked up by itself. Whatever falls or is poured into the funnel comes out of the outlet, and anything arriving faster overflows the quarter-flask funnel. You can loop one back, for example from a separator's outlet into its own tank.
  - Everything that moves fluid runs on **sim time**, interleaved with the chemistry: tools, hoses, drops, faucets and your own pouring. So a slow drip into a reacting flask comes out the same at any sim speed, a faucet keeps pace with the valve draining what it fills, 4× and 16× speed all of them up, and pausing freezes them (a flask held to pour tips, but doesn't pour while paused). So do the spectrometer's run, the receptacle's cycle, cooling, and the sounds of drops and streams. Nothing in the lab's chemistry or fluids runs in real time. What does: you (walking, looking, carrying, and tipping a flask), the arms, the ship's ambience (see **Sound**), and the emergency beacon, all of which carry on through pauses.
- **Scale** (`src/game/scale.ts`): let go of a flask just over its platform (no more than 60 above it) to stand it there, anywhere along it short of its ends, and pick it up again to take it off. Flasks on it move with the scale, and a spout above one pours into it, so you can dispense by weight. It reads whole grams up to 5 kg on a yellow-green seven-segment display on its front (OUEr beyond that), and its red key, unlabeled like the spectrometer's, tares it to zero. Fluid weighs 1 µg per atom, so a full flask of single atoms is 1 kg, and of pairs 2 kg. Each empty flask weighs about 100 g, off by up to 6 g. The error is fixed per shelf slot, so weighing fluid means taring with its flask first.
- **Supply and goal:** the cryostabilizer reference starts with a hundred flasks of the target, 300 billion atoms, and the intro's 2.811 L left is that, so a flask holds 28.11 mL. What counts is what the receptacle takes (see **Tools**): 5.023 L, the intro's sum (32,210 passengers × 0.000035 mL a day × 4,456 days), which is 178.7 flasks of the target by volume, flushed down its hose wins. That's one good load: a full receptacle more than 95% target holds at least 181 flasks of it, even if everything else in it is single atoms. The reference's hundred flasks are less than half of it. There's no progress bar; the receptacle's screen shows how far along it is. What's been delivered is saved with the lab, and a preset or Reset starts it again from nothing. The intro's numbers come from the same constants as the goal (`VOYAGE` in `src/game/config.ts`), so they stay in step.
- **God mode:** the checkbox in the header, on until it's first turned off. Whether it's on is kept in local storage beside the save (not in it, so exporting a lab doesn't carry it), so a refresh leaves it as it was. Looking at a flask or a tank, or holding a flask, shows by the crosshair its temperature, fill level, a species pie chart and the top species. The pie keeps a fixed order (singles, then pairs, then triples, counterclockwise from north), so its sectors don't jump around as amounts shift. The list is sorted by amount. Double-clicking one opens an editor for its temperature and composition, which lists every species in it, down to a single molecule, with its exact count. You can drag or type each number, add or remove any of the 50 species, or empty it. Its Equilibrate button brings the contents to full chemical equilibrium at their temperature at once, with every atom kept, using the same exact solver as the presets. That runs every reaction to its end, including ones the kinetics never get to, like blue bonds forming. If the result is more molecules than fit, the excess spills over the rim. Its Reactions button shows or hides a live list of what's reacting in it, busiest first: each reaction is netted against its reverse and shown the way it's going on net, with its rate in events per sim second. The top 15 are listed, with a count of the rest. The list stays shown or hidden from one opening of the editor to the next. It updates live while the contents react. Outside god mode, looking at a flask or tank shows only a blotch of its fluid's color, the same color it's drawn in, or a dashed outline if it's empty. Outside god mode, the Chemistry and Appearance panels are hidden, and the Sound panel has no spectrometer slider, since the name gives the machine away.
- **Sim speed:** pause, 1×, 4× or 16×, since Arrhenius waiting is boring.
- **Sound** (all synthesized with Web Audio, no audio files; the mixer is `src/game/audio.ts`). Browsers keep audio off until a click or key press, so it starts on the first one.
  - **Ship's ambience** (`src/game/ambience.ts`): from the moment the intro's console log fades (so through the wake-up notice) and on through the game, a warm low hum (partials of 48 Hz, beating slowly against near-twins, over low-passed brown noise, swelling gently), with a faint klaxon whooping (280 to 760 Hz over 1.3 s, every 2.2 s) and a faint fire alarm blaring in the temporal-three pattern (three half-second 520 Hz blares, then a gap, every 4 s), both muffled and echoing as if from down a corridor. It's atmosphere, not the lab, so unlike everything else it plays on in real time, paused or not. It's silent on the intro's start screen and during the log, including on a replay, and fades in over a couple of seconds.
  - **Drips** (`src/game/water.ts`): each drop that lands in a vessel plinks, a short tone around 700–1200 Hz that leaps upward as it dies. Drops that land on a counter or the floor are silent. At most one plinks every 0.07 s, so a crowd of drops at 16× doesn't turn into a buzz.
  - **Streams:** every vessel with a stream running into it (from a spout, a hose, a faucet, your own pouring, or an overflow spilling from above) trickles: band-passed noise that burbles unevenly, with little bubbles popping. It's as loud as the square root of the flow, full at a flask per sim second, and it rings at 240 / (1 − 0.9 · fullness) Hz, so it rises tenfold as the vessel fills, like a bottle under a tap. The six loudest streams are heard. Flow is measured per sim second, so sim speed doesn't change the sound, and pausing silences it.
  - **Spectrometer:** its rumble and chimes (see above).
  - **Receptacle:** its blips, verdict tones and flush (see above; `src/game/beeper.ts`).
  - **Where sounds come from** (`src/game/place.ts`): drips, streams, the spectrometer and the receptacle are heard from where they are relative to your head. Within 400 they play at full volume; further off they halve with every doubling of the distance, fading to silence at 6000. They pan toward the ear they're on, up to 0.8 of the way for something straight off to one side, and something straight behind you is 0.6 as loud as the same thing ahead. The ship's ambience comes from everywhere.
  - **Volume:** the *Sound* panel has a slider for each of these (hum, klaxon, fire alarm, drips, streams, spectrometer, receptacle) and one for everything. Gain goes as the square of the slider, so 50 plays a sound as synthesized, the top four times as loud, and the bottom silent. They start at 50, except the far-off klaxon at 23 and fire alarm at 26, and *Restore defaults* puts them back there. Sliders are kept in local storage, not in saves.
- **Presets:** the dropdown next to Reset loads a starting layout, and Reset restarts the current one. *Stranded* is the game. *Temperature range* shows flasks from T = 0 to T = 100, to show how temperature looks. *Heat exchanger demo* passes hot red and room-temperature green through the exchanger. *Separator demo* sorts red from cyan, into dispensers under the two end spouts. *Wash route (sandbox)* is a sandbox to tinker with: a hot separator of △RGY and blue with its two left spouts (all primary, and two in three) hosed back into its tank, hot blue dripping in from above, and a catch tank under the far right spout (all secondary). Picking it from the menu also restores the default chemistry. Presets are defined in `src/game/presets.ts`, and can place hoses by naming the tool spout and tank each end goes to. The dropdown shows only in god mode; outside it, Reset still restarts the current preset.
- **Chemistry table:** every parameter is live-editable. Drag a number sideways to scale it by 1% per pixel (100 px ≈ ×e). Double-click a bond energy `E` to flip its sign, or any other number to type it, which is how to set an `A` back to 0. *Restore defaults* puts the default chemistry back, volume back to counting molecules, and the drip, heater, cooling, separator, size sorter and flow meter settings back too. All reaction rates rebuild on every change.
- **Saving** (`src/game/save.ts`): the lab (every flask, tool, scale and hose, where each is and which way it faces, their contents (a heater's tube included), valves, tares and notes), the sticky notes, what's been delivered, where you stand and look, and the chemistry parameters are saved to local storage every 2 seconds and when you leave the page, and restored on load. Positions across and up are stored as on the old flat bench, with how far out from the back wall and which way things face added as optional fields, so older saves still load; anything outside the room is brought inside it. *Export* copies the whole setup as a string (base64 of JSON), and *Import* loads one. Contents are stored by species name, so saves survive reordering the species list.

## Open questions / next steps

- **Separation.** The separator and size sorter split species only partly. Candidates for more:
  - boiling: small species are volatile, which reuses temperature;
  - a size sieve: singles pass, pairs and triples stay;
  - a color trap: an item that binds one free color.

  The choice affects what shape the target should be.
- **Temperature control.** The resistive heater heats, and everything cools to the room. Nothing chills below room temperature yet except endotherms; an ice bath would. The *Temperature range* preset's flasks now drift to room temperature over a minute or two.
- **More glassware and tools.** Graduated cylinders and pipettes.
- **Tool follow-ups.**
  - A tank can only be emptied through its spout.
  - Tools can't stand on the scale, so a dispenser's contents can't be weighed.
  - Every atom weighs the same. Giving colors different masses would make the scale reveal something about composition.
- **Tuning.**
  - The swap prefactor: if it's too high, recoloring is trivial and the whole puzzle is getting the topology right.
  - Heat capacity: R + G alone heats up noticeably.
  - Whether the six colors stay distinguishable in mixtures.
- **Playtester notes** (not yet acted on):
  - Make sure there's a synthesis pathway: a route that actually reaches more than 95% cryostabilizer.
  - Maybe add an off switch to the new device. The note doesn't say which device is meant.
  - Maybe add a mixer.
  - Add some sort of UI tutorial on how to change valve positions (look at a valve, hold the right button and point).

## Development

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # local dev server
npm test           # chemistry tests (vitest)
npm run route      # a synthesis harness, printed stage by stage (src/game/route.test.ts)
npm run build      # typecheck + production build into dist/
```

Code layout:

- `src/chem/`: the chemistry model, pure TypeScript with no DOM. It covers atoms, species enumeration, parameters, and the reaction network with its integrator.
- `src/game/`: flasks, tools, the scale, pouring, fluid color, and `GameEngine`, which owns the lab: the player, input, collisions (`physics.ts`) and the simulation loop. `view.ts` draws it with Three.js, and `bodies.ts` says where each thing's parts are in the room. `route.ts` is a harness that runs a synthesis on the same pieces without the UI and reports each stage.
- `src/components/`: the React UI around the canvas: speed control, the hotbar, the crosshair's hints, the chemistry table and the god-mode panel.

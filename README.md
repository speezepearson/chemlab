# Slurry Lab

A first-person chemistry-lab puzzle game set in a world with much simpler chemistry than ours. You walk around a lab handling fluids in glassware, and the goal is to work out how to synthesize more of a scarce target fluid.

**Premise** (see the intro): you're Nadia Hassan, a passenger on a Celestia Starlines colony ship to Mu Ceti, woken from cryosleep after something hit the ship and took out the bridge, the crew and nearly all the cryostabilizer. The 32,210 passengers still asleep need 0.0003499 mL each a day of it to last the 4,456 days (12.2 years) left: 50.22 L; there are 28.10 L. The target species is the cryostabilizer, so you have to figure out how to make more. But first there's a smaller shortage to make up, as a warm-up: the antinucleant, R–B, a trace of which a day (0.0000641 mL) keeps each passenger free of ice crystals. The voyage needs 9.20 L of it, and there are 8.30 L. Once that's made, the lab's supply station is refitted for the cryostabilizer (see **Supply station**).

The faucets are unlimited, so the target is scarce because you don't know how to make it, not because you lack raw material. The puzzle is discovering a synthesis route.

You, Claude, are the owner and long-term maintainer of this codebase, and its design is up to you.

## The world's physics

### Fluids

A packet of fluid, such as the contents of a flask, is fully described by:

- its **temperature**, and
- its **count of each molecular species**.

Reaction rates depend on mole fractions, so ratios matter, not absolute amounts. Adding fluid gradually still matters, because it keeps one reagent scarce while it goes in.

There is a **solvent**, S (see **Atoms and molecules**): a clear atom that bonds with nothing, so it takes part in no reaction. It's there to dilute. Mole fractions are over all of a fluid's atoms, S's included, so S in a fluid slows everything that takes two molecules meeting (formation and displacement) by thinning them out, and leaves everything one molecule does alone (ring closure, ring opening, breaking). Diluted, a mix's equilibrium leans toward what's broken apart, since the pieces find each other less often. S also takes up room and soaks up heat like any atom, so a diluted fluid heats up less from its reactions, and is cooled or warmed as much by mixing. It costs volume and heat capacity, and nothing else.

Volume is measured in molecules, so a flask's capacity is a number of molecules: a billion. Capacity, fill level, pouring and flow all go by molecules, so bonding shrinks a fluid and breaking bonds swells it, and whatever no longer fits spills over the rim. A checkbox in the Chemistry panel counts atoms instead. Heat capacity, reaction rates, the god-mode percentages and the supply station's purity test stay per atom either way. The setting isn't saved, and *Restore defaults* turns molecule counting back on.

Every count in a vessel is a whole number of molecules, and its heat is a whole number of energy quanta (one unit of bond energy each). Temperature is derived as heat / (heat capacity × atoms), so mixing simply adds heat, and energy is conserved exactly, except what the heater adds and what's lost to the room (see **Heat**). Whenever the expected result is fractional, such as reaction events in a step, one species' share of a pour, or the separator's split, it's rounded at random: up with probability equal to the fraction. That keeps averages exact, so slow reactions still happen. JavaScript numbers hold whole numbers exactly up to 2⁵³ ≈ 9×10¹⁵, so plain numbers are enough and BigInt isn't needed. Faucet recipes are the one fractional thing: one atom's worth, poured out as whole molecules. Counts that large behave like continuous quantities, so no small-number artifacts show up. The chemistry doesn't care about the scale, since everything in it depends on ratios.

### Atoms and molecules

- **Five atoms:** R, G, B and K: red, green, blue and black, the four that bond; and S, the solvent, clear, which bonds with nothing (see **Fluids**). Black is drawn a near-black, a little blue, not pure black, so it isn't quite the black of a frozen fluid (see **Appearance**), though the two are close. S comes last in the atoms' order, so the four colors' bonds and species are just as they were before it.
- **Weights:** no two atoms weigh the same: R 6, G 3, B 4, K 5, S 2 (`ATOM_WEIGHT` in `src/chem/atoms.ts`). A molecule weighs its atoms' weights together. Weight shows only on the scale; heat capacity and everything else go per atom.
- **What a molecule is:** a connected graph on a set of distinct colors: at most one atom of each color, so no molecule can hold two Rs, and at most four atoms. Any two colored atoms can bond. S bonds with nothing, so it's only ever alone.
- **Species count:** 4 singles + 6 pairs + (4 color-triples × 4 connected shapes: 3 chains and a triangle) + 38 connected graphs on all four colors (16 trees, 15 with four bonds, 6 with five, and the one with all six) = 64, and lone S, the last, = **65 species**. That is small enough to simulate exactly.
- **Names** (`src/chem/species.ts`), which saves store, so each is unique: `R`, and `S` for the solvent; `R–G`; a chain of three `R–G–B`, its middle atom in the middle; a triangle `△RGB`. Of four: a chain `R–G–B–K`, along it, the end earlier in R, G, B, K order first; one bonded to the other three `R(G,B,K)`; a triangle with the fourth hanging off it `△GBR–K`, the atom it hangs from last in the triangle; a ring `□RGKB`, round it from R toward its earlier neighbor; a ring with one bond across it `□RGKB+R–K`; and all six bonds `⊠RGBK`. Atoms are otherwise in R, G, B, K order.

### Bonds

Each of the 6 color pairs (RG, RB, GB, RK, GK, BK) has three parameters:

- `E`: the bond energy. Positive means stable.
- `Ea`: the activation energy.
- `A`: the Arrhenius prefactor.

A species' energy is `U = −Σ E` over its bonds.

The default bond energies are round numbers, each times a random factor from 0.8 to 1.2, drawn once and written into `defaultChemParams` (redrawn until no two were within 5% of each other), so no two bonds have the same energy and each shows as its own line in the spectroscope.

### Reactions

Reactions run on sim time with Arrhenius kinetics. Units are `k_B = 1` and `T_room = 1`.

`Ea` is measured as the barrier's height **above the higher of the two states**. For a reaction with energy change `ΔU`:

- downhill rate = `A · exp(−Ea / T)`
- uphill rate = `A · exp(−(Ea + |ΔU|) / T)`

This gives detailed balance automatically, and `Ea = 0` is always legal. As a rule of thumb, a barrier of 5 is slowish (e^−5 ≈ 1/150 of A), a barrier of 15 is frozen, and a bond with `E = 8` is essentially permanent at room temperature but breaks readily at `T = 4`.

The reaction types are:

1. **Formation** (bimolecular). Two molecules with no color in common join with one new bond. The rate is `k · x_a · x_b`.
2. **Ring closure** (unimolecular). A bond forms between two atoms of the same molecule that aren't bonded yet.
3. **Breaking** (unimolecular). The reverse of 1 and 2: a bond breaks, leaving the molecule whole or splitting it in two.
4. **Displacement** (bimolecular, `Ea = 0`, one global prefactor, *displace A* in the Chemistry panel). A free single atom trades places with an atom bound in a molecule of two or more atoms that has no atom of its color, where one of the two is black, keeping the bond topology: free R, G or B displaces a bound K, as in `R–K + G ⇌ R–G + K` or `△RGK + B ⇌ △RGB + K`, and free K displaces a bound R, G or B, the same reactions the other way. Each direction has its own `ΔU`, from the species' energies. Two molecules never trade bound atoms, and a free atom never displaces anything from a molecule that already has its color. So displacement is fast recoloring through black, while formation is the slow construction of a shape, and the target's shape still has to be earned through formation.

S is in none of them: it neither bonds, nor displaces, nor is displaced. The network has 408 reactions (`ReactionNetwork.count`), as it had before S. The exact equilibrium solver carries S through untouched: conserved, alone, at no energy, though it counts in the mole fractions the rest balance by.

Each vessel's chemistry is stepped by explicit Euler along with everything else, every sim substep (at most 0.02 sim s), unless it's quiet (`ReactionNetwork.tick`). Each step works out how long the fluid takes to change appreciably: the shortest of how long any species in it would take to be used up by the reactions using it (gross, not net of the reverse, since that's what sets how far a step can go without overshooting), and how long the heat they give off would take to change their rates by a factor of e. Until 5% of that has gone by, up to 0.25 sim s, the vessel isn't stepped again, and then it's stepped through all the time it's owed at once. So no step moves more than about a tenth of any species, which follows the kinetics as closely as substeps do and is far too short to overshoot. It's stepped at once if the chemistry is edited, or if its temperature or any species' share of it moves its rates by more than 5% (fluid running in, a species appearing), in steps as long as the fluid as it is then allows, the first a single substep. Single colors, unreactive mixes and slow ones are stepped a few times a sim second; a mix of everything at room temperature every few substeps, and a hotter one more often, up to every substep.

### Heat

- **Reaction heat:** exotherms heat the fluid through a per-atom heat capacity.
- **Mixing:** heat adds, so temperatures average, weighted by atom count.
- **Heating:** besides exothermic reactions, the resistive heater (see **Tools**) heats whatever runs through it, and the swell bulbs' wire heats the little that's in them.
- **Cooling** (`src/game/cooling.ts`): fluid in every vessel (flasks, tanks, funnels, the instruments' tubes, the heater's tube, hose funnels) drifts exponentially to the room's temperature, T = 1, from above or below. Each sim second it closes 1 − e^(−rate · exposure) of the gap, where rate is 0.1 and exposure is a rough thermal surface per volume worked out from how the fluid stands as drawn: h deep, and w wide on average (its drawn area over its depth). Taking the fluid to be as deep front to back as it is wide, its floor and free surface give 2/h and its walls 4/w. So a thin layer, or a narrow column, loses heat fast, and a deep, wide pool slowly. A flask fills its wide base first; a tank fills straight up, a funnel up from its narrow stem, a pipette its tube and then its cup, and a swell bulb its bulb and then its neck; the heater's tube holds a shallow stream along its floor. All but 1/e of the gap goes in about 65 s for a full flask, 110 s for a full dispenser, 27 s for a full pipette, and 9 s for a tenth of a flask lying 2 deep across a dispenser's floor. Fluid in mid-air (streams, drops) doesn't cool. Faucets pour at T = 1, so faucet fluid just sits. Besides waiting, the ways to lower a temperature are diluting with faucet fluid, running endothermic reactions, or passing it through the heat exchanger against something cooler. The Chemistry panel's *Cooling* rows edit the rate and the ambient temperature; they aren't saved, and *Restore defaults* puts them back.

### Appearance

- **Color:** by default, a fluid looks like the atom-weighted mix of its colors, and the bond structure is invisible. This is lossy on purpose: R and G together average to a dull olive, for example, whether bonded or not. (The Appearance panel can try out colors that depend on bonds; see below.)
- **Clearness:** S is clear. It adds no color to the mix (the hue is the mix of the colored atoms alone), but thins the fluid: the more of its atoms are S, the more see-through it's drawn, by `solventAlpha` in `src/game/appearance.ts`: opacity 0.12 + 0.88 · (1 − share of S)^0.6, which falls slowly at first, so strong dilutions still show their color: about 0.70 at half S, 0.34 at nine tenths. Pure S is a faint, cool near-white at 0.12, still a liquid with a surface in its glass: a see-through fluid in a vessel thickens toward its edges, seen edge on, and shows a bright line where its surface meets the glass (`FLUID_EDGE` in `src/game/view.ts`), as clear liquid does; an opaque one is drawn as ever. Its surface itself isn't drawn: what's seen from above is the fluid's far side through its open top. So a full vessel's fluid is cut off at most 0.05 under the top of the solid it's drawn from (`FLUID_TOP`), rather than showing that solid's top as a lid, lit all across by the line, that a vessel a hair less full doesn't have (the station's reservoir, whose fluid is drawn just as tall as it stands, keeps its top). A see-through fluid is always drawn before the glass it's in, and before every other see-through thing, so the glass's shine is always seen over it. (Left to three.js, which draws see-through things farthest first by the middles of their shapes, a vessel's fluid and its glass, whose middles are nearly in the same place, swapped as the eye passed the level of those middles, and the fluid looked suddenly darker or paler.) This goes for everything fluid is drawn as: in vessels, streams, drops, hoses and the instruments' threads. Its faucet's valve's handwheel is clear-white glossy plastic, see-through only so far. Cold and heat darken and whiten it as any fluid.
- **Seen through a clear fluid:** what's behind a see-through fluid in a vessel looks bent, as through water in glass, where the same vessel empty shows it straight (`REFRACT` in `src/game/view.ts`). Each point of the fluid shows what's behind it shifted toward the fluid's middle by up to 14 world units at the fluid's distance, by how far the fluid's far side there turns away from the eye: so a round flask acts like a lens, bending most toward its edges, while a flat tank seen square on shows what's behind it nearly straight. The shift is full strength down from opacity 0.7 (half S) and fades in proportion to nothing as the fluid turns opaque, on top of what's behind showing less through a more opaque fluid anyway; an opaque fluid is drawn exactly as ever. It's still for a still scene. Only opaque things are seen bent: glass, and other see-through fluids, behind a clear fluid are seen straight, and something standing in the fluid can show a faint shifted copy of itself. To draw it, everything opaque is drawn a second time, at half the screen's resolution each way, once a frame and only while some see-through fluid is in view (three.js's transmission pass).
- **Temperature:** each effect is a smooth function of T, with no cutoff where one effect takes over from another (see `src/game/appearance.ts`).
  - **Cold** fluids fade toward black. The color's brightness is scaled by `1 − e^(−3T)`: black at T = 0 and almost full brightness by T = 1.
  - **Hot** fluids wash out toward white, by `min(1, ln(1+T) / ln(101))²`: barely at T = 1, about a quarter of the way at T = 10, and fully at T = 100. That's the only sign of heat: nothing glows around a hot vessel.
  - God mode shows the actual number.
  - The **Appearance** debug panel tunes every constant in these curves live. *Copy values* puts the current settings on the clipboard as JSON, and *Restore defaults* undoes your changes.
- **Composition, experimentally:** the Appearance panel can also make color depend on bonds, so reactions show. A fluid is then the atom-weighted average of its molecules' colors, and each molecule's color comes from one of three models:
  - *current*, the default: a molecule is the average of its atoms, so the fluid is the average of its atoms and no reaction changes it;
  - *1: paint*: a molecule's atoms multiply like paints, rescaled to their average's brightness, so R–G is olive and R–B purple;
  - *2: light*: a molecule's atoms add like overlapping spotlights, clipped, so R–G is bright yellow and △RGB white.

  *Mix* blends between the average (0) and the full model (1). The *Cloudiness* checkbox makes bigger molecules more opaque: the fluid's opacity is the atom-weighted average over its colored atoms of an α for singles, pairs, and molecules of three or four atoms, times the solvent's thinning.
- **Metal:** the god-mode *Appearance* panel's *metal* slider sets how the lab's metal is finished, from 0, fully matte, to 1, mirror-smooth; at 0.5 everything is as built, and it starts at 0.4, a little more matte than that. It sets the roughness of every metal surface: whatever's drawn at least 0.3 metallic, which is the tools' pipework and fittings, the clamp stands' rods, the faucets' chrome, the heaters' wire, the counters' steel tops and edges, drain grates and cabinet doors, the deck, the equipment cabinet, the supply station's plate, recess and shutter, the ceiling lights' frames, the porthole's rim, the blinds' button, and the airlock's walls, doors and cryopod shell; not the painted panels, glass, rubber, enamel, paper, fluids, walls or ceiling. Each goes in a straight line from roughness 1 at 0 to its own at 0.5, and on to 0 at 1. A surface with a roughness map has its map's roughness scaled instead, by 2 at 0 (as rough as it can be) to 1 at 0.5 and 0 at 1. Roughness changes at once; what reflects the metal (the room's reflections and the equipment wheels' icons) is drawn again once the slider's been still for 0.4 s. It's kept in local storage, not in saves, and *Restore defaults* puts it back (`src/game/finish.ts`, applied through `metal` in `src/game/textures.ts`).
- **Aim:** the god-mode *Appearance* panel's *Aim* slider sets how far off the crosshair a spout, a hose's end, a handwheel or a dial may be for C, Q and E to act on it (see **Hoses** and **Tools**): the half-angle of a cone round it, in degrees, from 1 to 30 in steps of 1, starting at 8. It's as seen at the normal field of view, and narrows as the view zooms in (its tangent times the tangent of half the field of view now over that of half the normal one, `aimCone`), so it always covers the same part of the screen. It's kept in local storage, not in saves (`src/game/aim.ts`), and the panel's *Restore defaults* puts it back to 8.

## The puzzle's chemistry

Blue bonds are uphill (E near −1 for now) and have no prefactor (A = 0), so they never form or break on their own, at any temperature. Blue gets into a molecule, or out of one, only by displacing black, or by free black displacing it. The target, the **△RGB triangle**, sits above R–G + B, so it never dominates an equilibrium.

Black bonds to red and green have a high barrier (Ea = 10), so at room temperature they hardly form or break, and it takes heat to bond black to anything or free it. That keeps a trace of free black from eating the target. Free black still displaces blue freely (displacement has no barrier), so each free black atom spoils one triangle into △RGK, freeing a blue. But that ring can't open cold, so the black stays stuck there instead of moving on to the next triangle. Hot, it does move on, and the damage keeps growing. The black–blue bond is uphill with A = 0 like blue's others.

The faucets pour single atoms only (and the solvent), so every molecule, the target included, has to be made by mixing. Blue can't be bonded to anything straight from its faucet, since blue bonds never form on their own: it gets in only by displacing black.

- **Stopcocks** (`Cock` in `src/game/tools.ts`, `STOPCOCK` in `src/game/bodies.ts`): what slows or stops a spout. A stopcock is a short inline fitting, hung straight down from a spout's tip: a dark square collar, 11 across, sleeving the tip from 6.5 above it to 1.5 below; a barrel of pipe 4 across down from that, 18.5 long, longer than the handwheel on it is across; and at its foot a tip like a spout's, 8 across and 6 tall, whose bottom, 26 below the spout's own tip, is where what the spout lets out now leaves it, so it adds that much length. On the barrel's front, halfway down it, is a plain valve's handwheel, just like a built-in one (see **Tools**), on a short dark hub, facing the tool's front. Its bar along the barrel is open, across it shut, and the spout lets out as far as it's open, at whatever rate it would fully open (see **Tools** for how a tool with several spouts shares that out). A spout with none is fully open. A spout has two places for one, each holding at most one, and a stopcock never goes on another: at its tip, and on the end of its hose, if the hose is out, hanging under the nozzle and facing the way the nozzle does, so that what the hose lets out leaves that stopcock's foot (the hose runs from the spout's tip, or from the foot of the stopcock there). With one in each, the spout lets out as far as both are open, their settings multiplied (`Tool.open`). It isn't a thing of its own in the lab: it's either on a spout or in your hands or in the cabinet, never set down anywhere else. The dispenser, the pipette, the big tank and the swell bulbs come out of the cabinet with one fitted on each spout, shut (`ToolShape.fitted`), so they hold what's poured in until they're opened; every other tool comes bare. A faucet's spout takes one too, at its tip (hanging from its chrome tip, facing as its own valve does) or on its hose's end, and so does the supply station's reservoir's faucet: either then lets out as far as its own valve is open times as far as its stopcocks are. The station's waste spigot takes none (only its diverter works it), and through a changeover nothing on the station can be fitted, turned or taken off; a stopcock on its faucet stays with it through the changeover.

Away from any spout, the left button only says "Put it on a spout". The left button anywhere on it (its handwheel included) takes it off into your hands, and the spout is open again. All of it is a part of its own for the outline, and it's taken in by its tool's faint outline. A hose pulled out of a spout with one at its tip runs from the stopcock's foot, which stays where it is. One on a hose's end is worked the same way, by itself, its foot included (the left button anywhere on it but its handwheel takes it off); with the crosshair on the spout's tip a stopcock in hand goes at the tip, and near the nozzle, on the hose's end, whichever of the two is free. There's no room for one under a nozzle docked on an inline instrument's tube; a nozzle with one on its end can still dock there, if the spout's tip has none and has room for it, which is where it goes, doing the same; otherwise it doesn't dock. When you roll the hose back up (Backspace, or Q on one just pulled out), a stopcock on its end goes up to the spout's tip if there's none there; if there is, it comes into your hands, set as it was, as one fresh from the cabinet would (Q or Backspace puts it in the cabinet), and once it's put somewhere, one undo brings back the hose and the stopcock on its end. When the hose goes some other way (the station's changeover, a load or an undo that leaves none out) and the tip has one already, the end's goes back in the cabinet (or is gone, if its compartment's full). A tool put away takes both with it. A nozzle with one on its end bumps into things down to the stopcock's foot, and held over an open top it goes as much higher as the stopcock needs.

Fitting one, taking one off and turning one are each one step to undo. A save keeps each spout's stopcocks, at its tip and on its hose's end, and how each is set (a save from when a spout had one, on either end, loads it on the hose's end if the hose is out, else at the tip); a save from before there were stopcocks has a tool that had flow valves of its own (a tank's, or a column's tap) get one fitted on each spout one of those let out of, set as that valve was, so a line runs on as it did (`OLD_FLOW` in `src/game/engine.ts`), and loses the valves (the swell bulbs keep their dial).
- **Notes:** press **Enter** on anything that can be carried (a flask, a tool, a scale), or a faucet, to write a note about it: the mouse is let go, and you type up to 24 characters, then Enter (once it's been let go of and pressed again) or click anywhere else to keep it, or Escape to leave it as it was. An empty note clears it. Notes float above what they're about, always facing you. A flask's note is its label, just above its mouth (and following it when it's carried). On a tool with one tank, the note is that tank's label, just above the tank, above the tool's own label if it has one; on the heat exchanger, N on a tank labels that tank, and anywhere else on it writes a note for the whole exchanger, above both; a thermometer or a wicking strip, which has no tank, has a note of its own, above its top. - **Tools** (`src/game/tools.ts`, and `src/game/bodies.ts` for where their parts are in the room) are glass tanks and pipes, built from their flat outlines on the old bench. Their glass stays nearly clear but reflects the room in full, more so edge on, as real glass does. A tank's fluid is drawn a hair inside its glass walls, resting on its floor, and the walls' shine shows over it seen at any slant, while the floor never flickers through the fluid lying on it; pipes are stainless, cabinets light powder-coated with rounded edges (as are the scale and the stands' bases), and handwheels' rims and bars black plastic. Only what's drawn of a tool counts as the tool (its glass included, however faint): the crosshair passes through the empty space around its tanks, pipes and handwheels to whatever's behind. A tank's glass and rim count as the tank, not just the fluid in it, so an empty tank can be looked at as one (for a note, the info panel, or a hose's nozzle to go over), and the supply station's funnel counts as a tank of its own. Each has tanks on top that hold four flasks' worth (the funnels of the multiplexer, size sorter and heater, the instruments' tubes and the swell bulbs are smaller). Every vessel is drawn just big enough inside to hold what it holds, as much room per atom as a flask's inside (a flask holds a billion in about 63,000 cubic world units), so how much fluid each looks to have agrees from one to the next: each tank is as deep front to back as it needs to be for its outline (about 50 for a four-flask tank, 30 for a funnel, 6 for a pipette's tube), and its fluid settles into it as drawn, filling a funnel's narrow stem, a sump or hopper, or a pipette's tube first. How fast a tank drains still goes by how high its fluid stands in its flat outline, as before. Fluid leaves each through one or more spouts on the bottom, each a short stub of pipe down from what it drains, 12 below it (`SPOUT_STUB`), ending in a little square tip. A tool has no valves of its own to slow what it lets out: a spout with nothing on it is fully open, and what slows or stops it is a stopcock fitted on it (see **Stopcocks**); the dispenser, the pipette, the big tank and the swell bulbs come with one fitted on each spout, shut. A tank lets out its spouts' openness times its full flow (1 flask/s), times how high the fluid stands as a share of the tank's height: a full tank pours at the full rate and a quarter-full one at a quarter of it. So a tank slows as it drains. One tank feeding several spouts (the separator's and the size sorter's) draws on itself as far as the most open of them is, shares that out as it does, and puts back what each spout, less open than that, doesn't pass (`throttle` in `src/game/tools.ts`, in whole molecules): with all of them as open as each other it lets out just what one valve over all of them would, and with one shut, what would have gone out of it stays in the tank, so the tank comes to hold more and more of it, and the others' streams thin out as it does. Flat-bottomed tanks (the dispenser's, the exchanger's, the separator's and the big tank's) have a shallow hopper under their whole floor instead of a flat one: as wide and deep as the tank, it narrows to a small tip (4 across) at the middle of the floor's front edge, so the tank's front wall runs straight down to the tip and its floor slopes down to it from the back and both sides (8 deep under the 52-wide tanks, 20 under the big tank). That's where it drains: the pipe from the tip runs down just inside the front wall, and all the tool's plumbing under its tanks (pipes, the exchanger's double helix, the separator's manifold, and the spouts) is there, at the front, not under the tank's middle; a hopper leaning forward is as wide and deep at each height as an upright one, so it holds just what it would. The height counts from its tip. A thin layer across the floor still has the hopper's depth behind it, so it keeps draining steadily, and once only the bottom of the hopper is wet, its level drops fast for how little is left, so the tank empties in a finite time instead of trickling on for ever. A full dispenser fully open is empty in about 14 s. The fluid is drawn filling the hopper first. A few tools have valves built in, which don't slow a spout but share out or set something: the multiplexer's selector, the heater's tap's valve, and the supply station's faucet's. Each is a handwheel mounted almost directly on its pipe, facing the tool's front (`VALVE_WHEEL` in `src/game/bodies.ts`): a disk 9 in radius and 3 thick, standing 1.5 proud of the pipe on a short dark hub, with ten rounded lobes round its black rim for grip, its face inside the rim a light, matte off-white that glows very faintly, so what's on it shows in any light, and across its face a raised black bar along a whole diameter (or for a selector, a pointer from its middle). A plain valve's bar says how it's set: along the pipe (upright) it's fully open, square to it (level) it's shut, and so it is within 10° either side of square (`VALVE_SHUT` in `src/game/bodies.ts`), so it needn't be turned exactly square to stop the last drop; beyond that it opens evenly, by how far it's turned, to fully open along the pipe (so a bar at 45° is a little under half open, 0.44). It turns freely all the way round, so it opens and shuts twice a turn, a quarter turn clockwise from open to shut and on to open again. With nothing in your hands, hold **E** to turn a valve's handwheel (or a dial) clockwise, or **Q** anticlockwise, as you see it (from behind, as you'd see it from there). Which one: the one the crosshair is on (its handwheel or the hub it's on; not the pipe beside it); or else, of every handwheel and dial that can be worked (a tool's built-in valves and dials, the stopcocks at its spouts' tips and on its hoses' ends, a faucet's valve and stopcocks, but nothing on the station through its changeover), the one whose middle is fewest degrees off the crosshair, within the same aim as C's (8° to start, see **Aim**) and within reach (460), and not hidden behind anything else, as for C (see **Hoses**), so the crosshair needn't be right on it (right on it, nothing hides it). It turns slowly at first, 40° a second, so a tap moves it only a few degrees, then faster, evenly up to 240° a second over the first 0.8 s the key's held (`TURN_RATE` in `src/game/engine.ts`): it's your own doing, like walking, so it goes in real time, paused or not. With both keys held it doesn't turn, and letting one go starts the other's turning slowly again. It's taken hold of only by a fresh press of either key while you look at it (so E still held from pouring doesn't turn a handwheel the crosshair comes onto), and it stays the one turned while it's within the aim, though another come nearer, until it's let go of: once neither key is held, or it's out of the aim or comes to be hidden (the crosshair not right on it), or something comes into your hands: from taking hold to letting go is one step to undo, if it changed. It's drawn over everything (a stopcock's collar, right over its handwheel, included), and it's nothing to aim at, it's never outlined, and it casts no shadow nor stops the shadow lamp's light. Far off it's drawn bigger, so that its letters are never less than 12 CSS pixels tall; and while a key's held its side is drawn white and the other faint. Plain valves start closed, the bar level.   - **Spouts** pour into the first open top straight below them (or below their hose's nozzle, if it's pulled out; see **Hoses**), in a stream whose width goes as the square root of its flow. Below 5M atoms/s (0.005 flask/s of single atoms) an outlet drips instead: what leaves it gathers in a hanging drop, which falls as a Poisson process whose rate, e^((atoms − size) / spread) per second, rises without limit as the drop grows: by default (size 1.5M, spread 0.12M, both set in the Chemistry panel) it's 0.015 per second at 1M atoms, 1 at 1.5M and 64 at 2M, so drops come out a fairly regular size however fast they're fed. Once it lets go, a drop falls under gravity into the first open top below. Hanging drops are saved; drops in mid-air aren't, and neither kind reacts. The open top can be a flask, including one you're holding under the spout, or another tool's tank, or the face of a sheet of test paper (see **Test paper**): anything whose mouth is under the spout, front to back as well as across. If there isn't one, the fluid falls to the counter or the floor and drains away. A stream's width shows everything that left the outlet over the whole frame, so a wide-open spout that drains its tank faster than it's fed still shows its stream at any sim speed.
- **Overflow:** fluid pouring into a full vessel (from a spout, a drop, a faucet, a hose or your own pouring) still goes in and mixes, and the same amount of the mixture spills over the rim: off the right of a flask's lip, or over the right lip of a tank or funnel, as it faces. It falls as a stream, into the first open top below, which may overflow in turn, or down the drain. So a flask of blue under a stream of red turns redder and redder while purple, then red, pours off it. Faucets keep running into a full vessel, too. Chemistry that swells a fluid (by molecules) spills the same way. A sheet of test paper is no vessel: it soaks up only what it has room for, mixing nothing more in, and the rest is lost (see **Test paper**).

  - **Resistive heater:** a horizontal glass tube, two cells long, with a copper wire coiled down its middle.     - **Shadows.** Whatever's in the channel between the pane and the screen is in the light's way, set down or held there in your hands. Anything opaque stops it all: a scale, a clamp stand's rod and clamp, a valve, a stopcock, a pipe, a heater's wire. Fluid dims it, whatever the fluid looks like, by one thing only: the share x of its molecules that have a ring in them (`ringShare` in `src/game/tools.ts`, `RINGED` in `src/chem/shape.ts`: a triangle, with or without a fourth atom hanging off it, and every four-atom molecule with four bonds or more; each molecule counts once whatever its size, and the solvent's atoms are molecules too, so diluting lowers it). Through a length L of fluid, in world units, e^(−0.2 · x · L) of the light gets through (`lampLight`, `LAMP.dim`), and fluids one after another each take their share. So a full flask of nothing but the triangle, about 50 across inside at its base, lets through e^−10, nothing, and casts a shadow as dark as a solid thing's, all but a thin fringe at its very edge, where the light has little of it to cross; one with 10 rings in a hundred molecules lets a third through the middle of its base and two thirds through its narrow neck and toward its edges, a shadow plainly darker through its middle than round it; one with 3 in a hundred lets three quarters through its base, a faint shadow but one that shows, a quarter of the way from the screen's bright green down to its dark; one in a hundred casts none that can be made out; and a flask of chains of the very same atoms, or of anything else with no ring, casts no shadow at all, however long it's looked for.     - **How it's worked out** (`Exposer` in `src/game/view.ts`): from what's drawn, so that what casts a shadow is exactly what's seen, with no second model of where things are. While the lamp's lit, or any of its screen is still fluorescing, the engine only adds up the sim seconds the screen has yet to catch up by (`Tool.owed`), never more than 7, by when it's all the way there. Each frame the lab's drawn, the view draws what's in the beam between pane and screen again, as seen looking along the channel from the pane with no perspective, every line of sight running the same way (which is what a beam of parallel rays sees), onto a picture with a pixel to each of the screen's 64 by 64 cells: everything opaque as in the way, and nothing see-through at all. Fluid is drawn so as to measure the light's way through it: whatever draws fluid marks its material with the fluid's share of rings and the height of its surface (`shade`), and each face of the fluid's solid adds to its pixel how far along the line of sight it is, the solid's far side as it is and its near side taken away, times 0.2 x, so a pair of faces leaves the dimming of the length between them, and all the fluids in the way add up. A vessel's fluid is drawn as a solid cut off at the fluid's level, with nothing across the cut, so a line of sight that goes in or out through the surface would meet one face without its pair; so each face is measured from where its line of sight crosses the level of the surface (taken no further off than the solid reaches), which changes nothing for a pair and pairs a lone face with the surface. That takes pictures that hold (half) floats; on a graphics card that can't draw to those, each body of fluid in the way counts as 40 long instead, however far through it the light goes. It does that several times (2 to 24 a frame, once to every 0.008 sim s owed, in a fixed sequence carried on from frame to frame), each looking a little off straight along the channel, within how far the beam's rays stray, and through its cells a little off their middles, and averages them, which is what softens and smooths the shadows' edges; then the screen's cells each close on that average by the time owed. With the lamp out, the screen just fades by the time owed, with nothing to work out, and once it's dark nothing's owed at all. So the screen follows the channel as it stood when each frame was drawn. While the lab isn't drawn at all (asleep in the pod, or napping) what the screen is owed waits, and on the first frame after, it's caught up with the shadows as they stand then.

  - **What smells:** only small molecules are volatile: the four colors' free atoms and the six pairs, ten odorants (`ODORANTS`). The solvent has no smell, nor has any molecule of three atoms or four, so a clean batch of the cryostabilizer smells of nothing, and any smell on it is its impurities'. An empty vessel smells of nothing. Each odorant smells of one thing, Nadia's own simile for it, from school and home: R is wet coats, G floor wax, B a struck match, K a swimming pool; R–G pencil shavings, R–K chalk dust, G–K new sneakers; R–B overripe banana, G–B cough syrup, B–K bubblegum. They're assigned so as to give nothing away: none goes with its species' colors, and a pair's has nothing to do with its two atoms', so bonding shows as two smells going and a third coming, and nothing in the words says which. One kinship is written in, for an attentive player to find: the three pairs with a blue atom are cloyingly sweet, each a different sweet thing, and the other three are dry or plain. That's authored against the default chemistry, whose blue bonds are the uphill ones; it's in the table, and doesn't follow edits to the chemistry.
    - **Ways of saying each:** she has a few ways of saying each one (`Odorant.similes`), and one is picked at random at every sniff, so it reads as a nose and not a gauge. Each is said as a thing ("Reeks of a struck match.") or, in the phrasings that end in "smell", as a sort of smell ("Concentrated struck match smell."), given here in brackets where it differs:

      - G: *floor wax*, *waxed floors* (*waxed floor*), *a freshly waxed hallway* (*waxed hallway*).
      - B: *a struck match* (*struck match*), *matches* (*match*), *a just-lit match* (*just-lit match*).
      - K: *swimming pool*, *the pool at school* (*school pool*), *pool water*.
      - R–G: *pencil shavings*, *a pencil sharpener* (*pencil sharpener*), *sharpened pencils* (*sharpened pencil*).
      - R–B: *overripe banana*, *banana*, *a banana left in a lunchbox* (*lunchbox banana*).
      - G–B: *cough syrup*, *cough medicine*, *a spoonful of cough syrup* (*cough syrup*).

  - **How strong:** an odorant's concentration is its share of the fluid's molecules, the solvent's counted, so diluting weakens a smell, and how much fluid there is doesn't matter: a film on a flask's floor smells as the flask did full. Each odorant has its own threshold, the share at which she first smells it at room temperature, and they span three orders of magnitude: K 0.0001, R–B 0.0003, B–K 0.0005, G–K 0.0015, G–B 0.003, R 0.02, R–K 0.02, B 0.025, G 0.06, R–G 0.1. Free black is the most potent, smelled at one molecule in ten thousand, because a trace of it is what spoils the cryostabilizer and nothing else in the lab shows a trace well; the antinucleant, R–B, comes next, since it's the first thing to be found. What's waded through in bulk (the faucets' R, G and B, R–G, and the R–K the antinucleant is made from) is weak, so it doesn't drown the rest. An odorant's level is how many factors of 10^0.75, about 5.6 (`STEP`), it is over its threshold, and the level picks one of five bands (`BANDS`, `bandOf`), which her words and their pacing go by (see **What she says**): the faintest from the threshold, the next from 5.6 times it, the middle one from 32 times, the next from 178 times, and the strongest from 1000 times up. So R from its faucet smells of wet coats (the middle band) and G is only a whiff of floor wax, while the antinucleant from the reservoir's faucet reeks of overripe banana.
  - **Warmth:** a warm fluid gives off more and a cold one less. Every odorant's concentration is multiplied by `exp(1.5 × (1 − 1/T))` (`waft`, `VOLATILITY`), as a vapor's pressure goes: 1 at room temperature, 0.22 at half of it and nothing at zero, 2.1 at twice it, and leveling off at 4.5 however hot, a little under one step. It's the same for all of them, so warming a fluid strengthens its smells together and never changes which masks which.
  - **Masking:** a strong smell wipes out much fainter ones. Only what's within 2 steps of the strongest (`MASK`: a factor of 32 in multiples of their thresholds) is made out, and never more than three (`NAMED`), strongest first. So a flask that reeks of one thing hides a trace of another. Thinning it doesn't help, nor warming it, since either changes all its smells alike: what's masked has to be found by taking the fluid apart. Her nose doesn't tire, either: a second sniff of the same fluid makes out just what the first did, whatever was sniffed in between. The thresholds are set so that the first goal's route can be found by nose. R–K and B mixed one to one, cold, make only about 3% R–B, with about 3% free black beside it, and that reads, put one way, "Smells strongly of swimming pool.", and under it "…with banana." and "…and a whiff of… chalk dust?": a strong smell, a plain one and a slight one, however they're put R–B is only three times less potent than free black, well inside the mask, so that much of it is still named beside three times as much free black, or at half as much itself, or with a few percent of B–K beside them (then the three named are swimming pool, overripe banana and bubblegum, in that order).

So the same flask is put differently from one sniff to the next, though what she makes out in it, and how strong, is always the same. What has no smell at all (the solvent, a clean batch, an empty flask, anything all under its thresholds) is one of (`NOTHINGS`): *Nothing.*, *No smell.*, *Nothing at all.*, *Doesn't smell of anything.*

The faintest band doubts there's anything there: *a trace*, *the faintest hint*, *maybe*, *barely*, *almost nothing*. The next is slight but there: *a whiff*, *a little*, *slight*, *notes of*. The middle one just names the smell, with no word for how strong. The next always says *strong* or *strongly*. The strongest *reeks*, *stinks*, or is *concentrated* or *overpowering*. No phrasing uses another band's words. And in the two faintest bands she trails off, hesitates before the name and ends on a question, while the three strongest are said flatly, all at once.       - strong: *Smells strongly of X.*, *A strong smell of X.*, *Strong x smell.*

    - **Of those after it** (`FOLLOWS`), each by its own band, where "with" is said of the second thing and becomes "and" of the third:

      - slight: *…with notes of…|X?*, *…with a whiff of…|X?*, *…with a little…|X?*, *…with a slight smell of…|X?*

    - **Pacing** (`PACE`): it comes up a piece at a time, in real time, from the moment her nose reaches the rim, and the fainter a smell is, the longer she takes over it, so the pacing says how strong it is as much as the words do. In the three strongest bands the whole line comes up at once. The pie keeps a fixed order (by size, singles first, counterclockwise from north), so its sectors don't jump around as amounts shift. The list is sorted by amount. Double-clicking one opens an editor for its temperature and composition, which lists every species in it, down to a single molecule, with its exact count. You can drag or type each number, add or remove any of the 65 species (S in a pale near-white, there and in the pie), or empty it. The total beside its temperature can be dragged or typed too, which scales everything in it in proportion, up to what fits, keeping its temperature. Its Equilibrate button brings the contents to full chemical equilibrium at their temperature at once, with every atom kept, using the exact equilibrium solver (`src/chem/equilibrium.ts`). That runs every reaction to its end, including ones the kinetics never get to, like blue bonds forming. If the result is more molecules than fit, the excess spills over the rim. Its Reactions button shows or hides a live list of what's reacting in it, busiest first: each reaction is netted against its reverse and shown the way it's going on net, with its rate in events per sim second. The top 15 are listed, with a count of the rest. The list stays shown or hidden from one opening of the editor to the next. It updates live while the contents react. Outside god mode, looking at a flask or tank shows nothing by the crosshair: what's in it is there to be seen in it. On a narrow screen (560 CSS pixels or less, as on a phone), each panel opens across the screen, 16 from either edge, rather than hanging off its left edge from its name. Outside god mode, the Chemistry and Appearance panels are hidden.
- **Sleeping** (`src/game/sleep.ts`): with the airlock open (see **The airlock**), walking into the pod, with the shell's footprint, is getting into it. Whatever she held is set down if it can be, or else put away, and from then on nothing works, not even looking around: she eases into place in the pod, turning the short way round to face the lab, her eyes a little down, over 1.4 real seconds (it's her own movement, like walking, so it isn't on sim time). Then the pod's door swings shut in front of her over 1.6 sim s, shutting out the room's light, and then everything fades to black over 1.5 sim s. While she sleeps the screen stays black but for two dim lines at its foot saying how long she's slept, in sim time as days, hours, minutes and seconds ("asleep · 00:03:12:05"), and what's in the ship's reservoir of what's needed, as the station's screen shows it ("cryostabilizer 43.88 L / 50.22 L"); the lab isn't drawn, and its sounds fall silent, though the ship's ambience plays on. The lab runs on as fast as it can, whatever the sim speed (pausing still pauses it), in slices of 12 ms of real time, between which the page gets on with anything else, so it stays responsive. It's run as ever, as a black box, for 20 sim s windows, in which every vessel's contents are measured; where they're changing steadily, the lab then jumps ahead, every vessel moved on as its changes had it moving (along a parabola where a rate is clearly changing from one window to the next), in whole molecules and quanta, and the lab's clocks moved on with it, for as long as nothing would happen that a window couldn't have seen: no vessel nearer its brim than half a window, no drained vessel changed by more than a tenth (a fiftieth while how its rate is changing isn't clear), no rate changed by more than a fifth, no further than the goal, and never once the reservoir is filling slower than the passengers use it up (see below), nor past when it will be. Then it settles for 5 sim s, is measured again, and so on. Drops in the air, drops hanging, the instruments' readings and the like are left as they were through a jump, since they're steady too. So a steady line runs hundreds of times faster than real time: the user's own line slept 7 h 26 min to the goal in about a minute in a browser (39 s in `npm run bench -- prod --mode=sleep --load=bench/saves/line.json`, against 29× real time run as ever), with its delivery the same within 0.04%. A line still settling, or one running down, gets less. The sleep ends when the station stops delivering: nothing let into the reservoir for 3 sim s, as when a supply runs out or the line turns impure, or over any sim minute, less cryostabilizer than the passengers use up in that time, so a line too slow to keep them alive doesn't count as delivering. That's the intro's 32,210 passengers × 0.0003499 mL a day, taking a sim second for a second: about 11.3 mL a day, or 464 molecules of the target a sim second (`SUSTAIN` in `src/game/config.ts`); through a jump, the reservoir's taken to have filled evenly. Either always happens in a window, run as ever. It also ends when the reservoir holds the goal (see **Supply and goal**). Either way she wakes in the pod as she did at first, the screen fading in from black over 1.5 real seconds (the lab waits, as it does under the message, so this isn't on sim time). If the station stopped delivering, the message is the one she woke to at first (see **Intro**), its figures brought up to date with the litres in the reservoir now, and OK goes on as it did then: the message flickers out, the door swings open, she walks out, and the doors close behind her, since nothing's being delivered. If there's enough, the message says only "Welcome to Mu Ceti." (see **The end**). Sleeping isn't saved: a reload while she sleeps finds her awake in the lab, with the airlock shut.
- **Napping:** the first time the supply station has delivered steadily for 20 sim s on end (see **The airlock** for what counts; `DELIVERY.nap`), whatever it's fitted for, a note comes up near the top of the view for 9 seconds: "Steady delivery. Press N to nap while it runs." From then on (it's saved with the lab; a preset or Reset forgets it), **N**, with empty hands, naps where you stand, whatever the crosshair is on (notes used to be on N, and are on Enter so that it needn't be on nothing), any time but during the station's changeover; the corner list of actions says so. Your eyes close over 0.9 real seconds (it's your own doing, like walking): the upper lid comes down and the lower up to meet it, soft-edged, the view dimming between them. With them shut the screen is black, the lab isn't drawn, its sounds fall silent, and it runs on as fast as it can, jumping ahead while it's steady, exactly as while asleep in the pod (see **Sleeping**), under the same dim lines at the screen's foot: "napping · 00:03:12:05", what's in the station's reservoir of what's needed ("antinucleant 8.71 L / 9.20 L"), and "N to wake". Nothing you'd do does anything but **N** again (or a click or tap on the black), which wakes you, whether or not the mouse is captured: the lab runs as ever at once, and your eyes open over 0.6 seconds. A nap also ends when the reservoir comes to hold enough of what the station is fitted for (unless it already did when the nap began): with antinucleant, that's the changeover's alarm going off; with cryostabilizer, it's the goal. Unlike sleep in the pod, a nap doesn't end because the station stopped delivering, so it runs on, at whatever speed the lab can be run, whether or not anything is being made.
- **The end:** OK on the welcome to Mu Ceti makes it flicker out, as at first; then the pod's door swings open, over 1.6 seconds, not onto the lab but onto a pure white void, so bright it blooms over everything, its light pouring into the pod as it opens: nothing is drawn but the pod, inside a sphere many times brighter than white, lit by white light from out in front and all round as far as the door is open, through a bloom and ACES tone mapping (`ARRIVAL` in `src/game/view.ts`). A second after it's open, the screen starts fading to white, all white 5 seconds after OK. Through all of it the lab stands still as it was when the reservoir reached the goal, so it goes in real time. On the white, in dark text: "Good work!" and "A chemist is you.", over two buttons. *Reset progress* puts the lab back as it was at first (as Reset does, with the reservoir's hundred flasks) and plays the intro again. *Continue tinkering* puts her in the lab as it was when there was enough, standing just out of the airlock facing the lab, with its doors shut, and the lab runs on from there. Reaching the goal while awake, by pouring, shows only a small note at the top of the screen, "Enough cryostabilizer to reach Mu Ceti."
- **Sim speed:** pause, 1×, 4× or 16×, since Arrhenius waiting is boring.
- **Sound** (all synthesized with Web Audio, no audio files; the mixer is `src/game/audio.ts`). Browsers keep audio off until a click or key press, so it starts on the first one.
  - **Ship's ambience** (`src/game/ambience.ts`): from the moment the intro's console log fades (so through the wake-up notice) and on through the game, a warm low hum (partials of 48 Hz, beating slowly against near-twins, over low-passed brown noise, swelling gently), with a faint klaxon whooping (280 to 760 Hz over 1.3 s, every 2.2 s) and a faint fire alarm blaring in the temporal-three pattern (three half-second 520 Hz blares, then a gap, every 4 s), both muffled and echoing as if from down a corridor. It's atmosphere, not the lab, so unlike everything else it plays on in real time, paused or not. It's silent on the intro's start screen and during the log, including on a replay, and fades in over a couple of seconds.
  - **Drips** (`src/game/water.ts`): each drop that lands in a vessel plinks, a short tone around 700–1200 Hz that leaps upward as it dies. Drops that land on a counter or the floor are silent. At most one plinks every 0.07 s, so a crowd of drops at 16× doesn't turn into a buzz.
  - **Streams:** every vessel with a stream running into it (from a spout, a hose, a faucet, your own pouring, or an overflow spilling from above) trickles: band-passed noise that burbles unevenly, with little bubbles popping. It's as loud as the square root of the flow, full at a flask per sim second, and it rings at 240 / (1 − 0.9 · fullness) Hz, so it rises tenfold as the vessel fills, like a bottle under a tap. The six loudest streams are heard. Flow is measured per sim second, so sim speed doesn't change the sound, and pausing silences it.
  - **Supply station:** as it's been delivering cryostabilizer steadily for a sim minute, and the airlock opens, it chimes a happy major chord, four bell-like dings rising from C5 to C6 a beat apart (`src/game/chime.ts`). Its diverter clunks as it swings, a little higher toward the reservoir than toward the waste (`src/game/diverter.ts`); what it lets into the reservoir goes in silently. Through its changeover (`src/game/changeover.ts`), an alarm sounds beside it, two harsh square-wave notes a fifth apart (622 and 933 Hz) through a band-pass, on for 0.45 sim s of every 0.9; the block and the door rumble as they slide (brown noise over a low growl, the block's the louder), and behind the shut door the machinery rumbles on, duller; and something heavy clunks as the block unlatches, as the door shuts, as the next block is set down behind it (duller), as the door lifts, and as the new block seats in the wall. All of it follows sim time, silent while paused.
  - **Thermometers:** one that shatters cracks sharply, a snap of high-passed white noise gone in a tenth of a second, and the shards tinkle after it, seven short high pings at random pitches (2.6 to 7.4 kHz) scattered over the next third of a second (`src/game/shatter.ts`). It's on sim time, like what broke it.
  - **Where sounds come from** (`src/game/place.ts`): drips, streams, the supply station (from its display) and a thermometer shattering (from its bulb) are heard from where they are relative to your head. Within 400 they play at full volume; further off they halve with every doubling of the distance, fading to silence at 6000. They pan toward the ear they're on, up to 0.8 of the way for something straight off to one side, and something straight behind you is 0.6 as loud as the same thing ahead. The ship's ambience comes from everywhere.
  - **Volume:** the *Sound* panel has a slider for each of these (hum, klaxon, fire alarm, drips, streams, station, glass) and one for everything. Gain goes as the square of the slider, so 50 plays a sound as synthesized, the top four times as loud, and the bottom silent. They start at 50, except the far-off klaxon at 23 and fire alarm at 26, and *Restore defaults* puts them back there. Sliders are kept in local storage, not in saves; one stored for a sound there no longer is (the composition analyzer's hum, see **Test paper**) is ignored.
- **Presets:** the dropdown next to Reset loads a starting layout, and Reset restarts the current one. *Stranded* is the game: nothing's out but the spectroscope, the station and the shadow lamp, and everything else is in the equipment cabinet. Every preset starts with the faucets shut. A preset's flasks stand along the front of the first island (the one nearest the back left corner), and the demos' tools over it, a cell and a half in from its back edge, their stands in its first row (a tool catching another's spouts stands as far forward of that as they are in front of their tool's middle, so they're over its tanks' middles). *Temperature range* shows flasks from T = 0 to T = 100, to show how temperature looks. *Heat exchanger demo* passes hot red and room-temperature green through the exchanger. *Separator demo* sorts red from black, into dispensers under its two spouts. Presets are defined in `src/game/presets.ts`, and can pull out hoses, naming the spout each comes from and the tank its nozzle goes over. The dropdown shows only in god mode; outside it, Reset still restarts the current preset.
- **Chemistry table:** every parameter is live-editable. Drag a number sideways to scale it by 1% per pixel (100 px ≈ ×e). Double-click a bond energy `E` to flip its sign, or any other number to type it, which is how to set an `A` back to 0. *Restore defaults* puts the default chemistry back, volume back to counting molecules, and the drip, heater, cooling, separator and size sorter settings back too. All reaction rates rebuild on every change.
- **Saving** (`src/game/save.ts`): the lab (every flask, tool, scale and hose, where each is and which way it faces (and how far a hose's stand is behind its nozzle), their contents (a heater's tube included), built-in valves, the stopcocks on their spouts and how each is set, tares and notes, each faucet's valve, stopcock and note, a thermometer's bulb's temperature and whether it's broken, what's on a wicking strip, what's on a column's bed and how far it has soaked, whether the shadow lamp is lit, what each sheet of test paper or bond paper has soaked up (and how far what it shows has come up, while it's still doing so), and what the supply station is fitted for and how far through its changeover it is), whether you've been told you can nap, where you stand and look, and the chemistry parameters are saved to local storage every 2 seconds and when you leave the page, and restored on load. Positions across and up are stored as on the old flat bench, with how far out from the back wall and which way things face added as optional fields, so older saves still load; anything outside the room is brought inside it. *Export* copies the whole setup as a string (base64 of JSON), and *Import* loads one. Contents are stored by species name, so saves survive reordering the species list. Sticky notes in saves from when there was a pencil are ignored.

## Open questions / next steps

- **Separation.** The separator and size sorter split species only partly. Candidates for more:
  - boiling: small species are volatile, which reuses temperature;
  - a size sieve: singles pass, everything bigger stays;
  - a color trap: an item that binds one free color.

  The choice affects what shape the target should be.
- **More instruments.** The thermometer, swell bulbs, wicking strips and the station's needle came out of one brainstorm (and dipping a strip straight into a vessel, like a thermometer, is built now); these from it aren't built:
  - a sleeper culture, which was built and then taken out again: a flat glass plate of living cells from the ship's stores, filled through a funnel on top and drained through a valve under it, on which a fluid was tried before it went near the passengers. The fluid killed the cells it covered, each at random and for good, the sooner the more loose ends it had per molecule (a lone atom counting as one, the solvent as none) and the hotter it was above T = 2, so the brown spreading through the pale blue cells said how harmful it was. An older save's is lost as it loads, with any hose pulled out of its spout. The code is in the history, at the commit that took it out;
  - ringing glass, which was built and then taken out again: a key that flicked the flask or tank looked at, which rang with the notes of what was in it. Each molecule's notes were those of its atoms on its bonds as springs (the atoms' weights, the bonds' energies as stiffness), brought down into hearing, each species' as loud as the square root of its share, so a chain among triangles was a stray note a tritone under them; and the glass's own notes fell as the vessel filled. It was shelved because the long ring it needs to be read by ear isn't how a full vessel sounds, which rings lower but duller. The code is in the history, at the commit that took it out;
  - stain tubes: a tube snapped onto a nozzle, whose stain creeps along in proportion to the loose ends that have passed, in a tint per end color, so a trace adds up over a whole flask;
  - witness coupons: a slip hung in a tank that lays down a band of tarnish each sim minute by how much free black there was, to read after sleeping;
  - a frost point: a slow chill in which a pure fluid frosts sharply and a mixture later, and the mixed test (reference, sample, and both together); it needs a chiller (below);
  - drop size by composition, so two pipettes dripping side by side drift out of step;
  - a seed crystal that grows in layers, clear when pure and specked by molecules of the same atoms in another shape;
  - froth whose bubbles are regular only for a single species, and a glow while something's reacting;
  - a getter cartridge that takes up free black until it's full (it would also purify, which changes the puzzle);
  - pinning dried wicking strips to the back wall.
- **Temperature control.** The resistive heater heats, and everything cools to the room. Nothing chills below room temperature yet except endotherms; an ice bath would. The *Temperature range* preset's flasks now drift to room temperature over a minute or two.
- **More glassware and tools.** Graduated cylinders and pipettes.
- **Tool follow-ups.**
  - A tank can only be emptied through its spout.
  - Tools can't stand on the scale, so a dispenser's contents can't be weighed.
- **Tuning.**
  - The displacement prefactor: if it's too high, recoloring is trivial and the whole puzzle is getting the topology right.
  - Heat capacity: R + G alone heats up noticeably.
  - Whether the four colors stay distinguishable in mixtures, and black from a cold fluid, which also fades to black.
  - The solvent makes hot size-sorting easier: a chain split apart hot doesn't find its pieces again as soon, diluted, so they can be sorted before they rejoin. Its cost in volume (what it takes up, and has to be separated out again) and in heat (what it takes to heat it with the rest) is the lever on how easy that is.
  - The four-atom chemistry's defaults are ported from the six-atom one by analogy (black in yellow's place) and not yet tuned into a verified puzzle.
- **Playtester notes** (not yet acted on):
  - Make sure there's a synthesis pathway: a route that actually reaches more than 95% cryostabilizer.
  - Maybe add an off switch to the new device. The note doesn't say which device is meant.
  - Maybe add a mixer.
  - Add some sort of UI tutorial on how to change valve positions (look at a valve or a stopcock and hold Q or E; an arrow over it now shows which turns it which way).

## Development

```sh
npm install
npm run dev        # local dev server
npm test           # chemistry tests (vitest)
npm run build      # typecheck + production build into dist/
```

Code layout:

- `src/chem/`: the chemistry model, pure TypeScript with no DOM. It covers atoms, species enumeration, parameters, and the reaction network with its integrator.
- `src/game/`: flasks, tools, the scale, pouring, fluid color, and `GameEngine`, which owns the lab: the player, input, collisions (`physics.ts`) and the simulation loop. `view.ts` draws it with Three.js, and `bodies.ts` says where each thing's parts are in the room.
- `src/components/`: the React UI around the canvas: speed control, the equipment wheels, the crosshair's hints, the chemistry table and the god-mode panel.
